\section{Experimental Validation and Testing}
\label{sec:validation}

This chapter evaluates the SAA and PAA on blocks excluded from policy-parameter fitting. Passive buy-and-hold references test return relative to the reward counterfactual (Section~\ref{sec:excess-return}). Signal-free and randomized-signal PAA ablations test whether the frozen SAA adds allocation value (Section~\ref{sec:info-flow}). These comparisons distinguish profitable allocation from a benefit attributable to signal transfer. Validation is used for selection. The final test is excluded from design decisions.

This chapter is organized in the order its two agents were introduced. Section~\ref{sec:oos-protocol} recaps the evaluation protocol shared by both agents and introduces the validation periods used. Section~\ref{sec:saa-results} reports SAA results on the validation periods. Section~\ref{sec:paa-results} reports the PAA results on both validation and test periods.

\subsection{Out-of-Sample Protocol}
\label{sec:oos-protocol}

The split in Section~\ref{sec:simulator} provides four historical validation blocks, each separated from its preceding training block by a 60-trading-day purge. A further 60-day purge separates the last validation block from the final test. A \emph{checkpoint} is a snapshot of learned parameters during training. Validation is consulted repeatedly to select checkpoints and inspect behavior. Its results therefore do not constitute a fresh test after model development. Fractional-differentiation calibration also includes pre-test validation dates (Section~\ref{sec:state-ffd}). Only validation-selected models are reported on the final test, which is reserved until design and selection decisions are fixed.


Table~\ref{tab:oos-blocks} describes the realized market context of each validation interval. Non-overlapping dates do not imply comparable regimes: rising markets can reward persistent exposure, whereas shocks can reveal its downside. Reporting each block separately therefore helps distinguish timing, asset selection, and risk reduction. The event narrative provides context, not proof that a policy responded to a particular announcement.

Four terms recur. \emph{Quantitative easing} (QE) denotes large-scale central-bank securities purchases intended to ease financial conditions \cite{FedAssetPurchasesSeptember2012}. A \emph{yield-curve inversion} means short-maturity government bonds yield more than longer-maturity bonds. The Treasury maturity spread records this relationship \cite{FREDTreasurySpread}. The Chicago Board Options Exchange's \emph{Volatility Index} (VIX) measures near-term S\&P 500 volatility implied by option prices \cite{BISVolatility2018}. A \emph{carry trade} borrows in a low-interest-rate currency to invest in higher-yielding assets elsewhere. Closing such positions during stress can amplify currency and asset-price movements \cite{BISCarryTrades2024}.

The test block is not used for narrative-driven model selection. Its portfolio outcomes are reported only after the validation decisions are fixed.

\subsection{Single-Asset Agent Results}
\label{sec:saa-results}


This section compares SAA runs \texttt{00304}--\texttt{00314}, configurations \texttt{01060}--\texttt{01070}, on all four validation blocks. Each evaluation covers all eleven assets from cash-only starts. Annualized return \(g_i\), also called compound annual growth rate, converts asset-account return to a 252-transition annual scale. For elapsed transition count \(n_i\), initial account value \(V_{i,0}\), and final account value \(V_{i,n_i}\),
\begin{equation}
g_i=\left(\frac{V_{i,n_i}}{V_{i,0}}\right)^{252/n_i}-1,\qquad
\alpha_i^{\mathrm{asset}}=g_i-g_i^{\mathrm{BH}},\qquad
\alpha_i^{\mathrm{market}}=g_i-g_{\mathrm{SPY}}^{\mathrm{BH}}.
\label{eq:saa-evaluation-alpha}
\end{equation}
Asset alpha \(\alpha_i^{\mathrm{asset}}\) compares annualized SAA return \(g_i\) with same-asset buy-and-hold return \(g_i^{\mathrm{BH}}\). Market alpha \(\alpha_i^{\mathrm{market}}\) uses SPY return \(g_{\mathrm{SPY}}^{\mathrm{BH}}\). These are return differences, not regression-based alphas. Their table values are percentage points.

Sharpe ratio \(\mathrm{SR}_i\) measures mean daily return relative to total variability. Exported Sortino ratio \(\mathrm{So}_i\) uses the standard deviation $\operatorname{sd}$ of negative daily returns only. For daily account returns \(r_{i,t}\), the table-generation utility computes
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{SR}_i=\sqrt{252}\frac{\operatorname{mean}(r_i)}{\operatorname{sd}_0(r_i)},\qquad
\mathrm{So}_i=\sqrt{252}\frac{\operatorname{mean}(r_i)}
{\operatorname{sd}_0(\{r_{i,t}:r_{i,t}<0\})}.
\label{eq:saa-evaluation-ratios}
\end{equation}
Population standard deviation \(\operatorname{sd}_0\) uses divisor equal to the observation count. Neither ratio subtracts the cash rate. Exported Sortino ratio \(\mathrm{So}_i\) differs from the smoothed downside-second-moment reward estimate \(S_t\) in Equation~\eqref{eq:sortino}. Maximum drawdown \(M_i\) is the largest decline from a preceding account-value peak, reported negatively in the SAA tables. Calmar ratio \(\mathrm{Ca}_i=g_i/|M_i|\) measures annualized return per unit of maximum loss. Action variation \(T_i^a=100\sum_t|a_{i,t}-a_{i,t-1}|\), starting with \(a_{i,-1}=0\), is the exported ``turnover'' measure. It measures changes in scaled action requests, not executed notional turnover.

Mean action variation \(\overline T^a=1622.64\%\) accompanies mean transaction cost \(\overline c^{\mathrm{tc}}=0.28\%\) of initial capital. Frequent signed trade bars in the asset plots confirm that the SAA actively resizes positions. Cash carry also differs by block. Since the SAA is long-only and can retreat to cash, part of asset alpha \(\alpha_i^{\mathrm{asset}}\) can reflect the prevailing EFFR rather than timing alone.

Val\_02 and val\_03 are retained for detailed plots because they contain contrasting declines, recoveries, and asset-specific trends. They are not the best and worst blocks by mean asset alpha \(\overline\alpha_b^{\mathrm{asset}}\). Aggregate plots show the equal-weight SAA account value, SPY buy-and-hold, and cash, indexed to 100 at the initial date. The JSON exports also provide equal-weight asset buy-and-hold returns. Asset plots show each SAA account, its own buy-and-hold reference, and signed dollar trading volume.

In val\_02, the equal-weight SAA portfolio initially stays close to its starting value, then falls sharply during the late-2018 decline before recovering (Figure~\ref{fig:saa-agg-val02}). Cumulative return \(R_{\mathrm{SAA},02}=1.41\%\) is below SPY's \(6.72\%\) and cash carry of approximately \(2.4\%\), but above equal-weight buy-and-hold's \(-3.81\%\). Aggregate maximum drawdown \(M_{\mathrm{SAA},02}=13.74\%\) shows that the SAA does not avoid the market shock.

The asset panels explain the positive asset-relative result despite this weak aggregate endpoint. EWG and EWS finish \(13.80\) and \(12.88\) cumulative percentage points above their own references (Figure~\ref{fig:saa-assets-val02-p1}). EWY has the largest cumulative advantage at \(15.85\) points, while Gold adds \(2.51\) points (Figure~\ref{fig:saa-assets-val02-p2}). Crude and SPY underperform by \(3.96\) and \(2.59\) points. Nine of eleven SAA accounts beat their own references, but no SAA account beats SPY on annualized market alpha \(\alpha_i^{\mathrm{market}}\). Signed trade bars indicate buys and reductions in long-only holdings, not short sales. Total cost across the eleven accounts is \(C_{02}^{\mathrm{all}}=\$249.75\).

\subsubsection{Discussion}
\label{sec:saa-results-discussion}

\label{sec:saa-robustness}

SAA run \texttt{00312}, configuration \texttt{01068}, has the highest mean asset alpha \(\overline\alpha^{\mathrm{asset}}\) and the largest positive-cell count, but not the highest mean Sharpe ratio \(\overline{\mathrm{SR}}\) or shallowest mean drawdown \(\overline M\). Its mean asset-alpha advantage over SAA run \texttt{00306} is \(0.65\) points. The trade-off is a deeper mean drawdown and greater action variation \(\overline T^a\). The eleven-candidate comparison therefore supports a return-based choice rather than unqualified risk-adjusted superiority.

The selected SAA remains regime-dependent. Mean asset alpha \(\overline\alpha_b^{\mathrm{asset}}\) is highest in val\_00 and negative in val\_01. Val\_02 contains widespread outperformance of each assigned asset, but the equal-weight SAA portfolio still trails SPY and cash (Figure~\ref{fig:saa-agg-val02}). In val\_03, the SAA portfolio beats both passive portfolio references at the endpoint while missing part of Gold's rally (Figures~\ref{fig:saa-agg-val03} and~\ref{fig:saa-assets-val03-p2}). Positive asset alpha does not necessarily imply that isolated timing beats the strongest available investment.

These outcomes follow from the SAA's action design in Section~\ref{sec:saa}. Scalar request \(a_t\) is a bounded position change scaled by \(\kappa_t\), not a target weight. It trades a fraction of current account value \(V_t\). The SAA is long-only, so its defensive response is to reduce or close its holding and earn cash carry, not to short a falling asset. Drawdown-aware reward \(\mathcal R_t\) can favor this risk reduction, while asset alpha \(\alpha_i^{\mathrm{asset}}\) records the opportunity cost when an asset continues to rally. Market-relative features inform each decision, but the SAA cannot reallocate capital to another instrument.

The hierarchical PAA can make that cross-sectional choice. Each frozen SAA account maintains isolated cash, holdings, and recurrent state \(h_t^{(i)}\) (Section~\ref{sec:info-flow}). SAA request \(a_t^{(i)}\) and shadow holding fraction \(\rho_t^{(i)}\) enter each asset token alongside live weight \(w_t^{\mathrm{live},(i)}\) and recorded target \(w_{t-1}^{(i)}\). Shadow Sortino \(S_t^{\mathrm{sh},(i)}\) and drawdown \(M_t^{\mathrm{sh},(i)}\) enter both token types (Section~\ref{sec:paa-state}). Attention can weigh this combined information against other assets rather than treating request \(a_t^{(i)}\) as an allocation instruction.

SAA run \texttt{00312}, configuration \texttt{01068}, is selected for hierarchical PAA training. Positive mean asset alpha \(\overline\alpha^{\mathrm{asset}}\) supports using its timing information, while the variation across blocks justifies leaving the hierarchical PAA free to discount it. The final test remains excluded until hierarchical PAA design and checkpoint choices are fixed.


\subsection{Portfolio Allocator Agent Results}
\label{sec:paa-results}

This section evaluates the hierarchical PAA, the AR(1) random-signal control PAA, and the cross-sectional-only PAA. An \emph{ablation} removes or replaces one component while retaining the surrounding system, so that its contribution can be assessed. Hierarchical PAA candidates are runs \texttt{00315}--\texttt{00318}, configurations \texttt{10027}--\texttt{10030}. The AR(1) random-signal control PAA candidates remain runs \texttt{00291}--\texttt{00294}, and the cross-sectional-only PAA candidates remain runs \texttt{00295}--\texttt{00298}. Results are presented first. Their implications for signal transfer and allocation are discussed separately.

The \textbf{hierarchical PAA} is the proposed two-layer system. It receives frozen SAA request \(a_t^{(i)}\), shadow holding fraction \(\rho_t^{(i)}\), shadow Sortino \(S_t^{\mathrm{sh},(i)}\), and shadow drawdown \(M_t^{\mathrm{sh},(i)}\), alongside market features and live account information. These quantities describe the SAA's decision and hypothetical account history without requiring the hierarchical PAA to obey it (Section~\ref{sec:info-flow}).

The \textbf{AR(1) random-signal control PAA} replaces the learned SAA action with synthetic noise. An autoregressive process of order one, abbreviated AR(1), constructs today's signal from a fraction of yesterday's signal plus a new random disturbance. Here the retained fraction is \(0.9\), and the disturbance has standard deviation \(0.15\). Clipping bounds the signal before the action scale is applied. Consequently, the noise is persistent rather than independently redrawn each day. It trades the same shadow accounts, producing corresponding exposure, Sortino, and drawdown histories. This replaces the source of the entire SAA-derived information stream, not merely one isolated scalar.

The \textbf{cross-sectional-only PAA} omits the learned SAA. Its action and shadow holding fraction are zero, and its shadow accounts are never traded. The shadow risk-summary fields remain, but describe those untraded cash accounts. They are not separately masked to zero. \emph{Cross-sectional} means comparing assets at the same decision date. The cross-sectional-only PAA retains market features, live statistics, and recorded targets, including historical summaries. It therefore removes learned SAA behavior, not all temporal or shadow-account information. Neither PAA ablation runs the frozen SAA. All three full-feature variants retain the same architecture and input dimensions.

\subsubsection{Results}
\label{sec:paa-results-presentation}

\paragraph{Selection criterion and evaluation conditions.}
Within each run, the saved peak-excess checkpoint is the candidate for comparison. One candidate per model family is then selected by the highest mean excess return over SPY across the four validation blocks:
\begin{equation}
J_{\mathrm{val}} = \frac{1}{4}\sum_{b=0}^{3}\left(R_{\mathrm{PAA},b}-R_{\mathrm{SPY},b}\right),
\qquad R_{k,b}=\frac{V_{k,b}^{\mathrm{end}}}{V_0}-1.
\label{eq:paa-selection}
\end{equation}
Every evaluation starts from \(V_0=\$100{,}000\), so maximizing this criterion is equivalent to maximizing mean dollar excess value. These are cumulative block returns, not the annualized alpha measures used in the preceding SAA tables. Each validation block receives equal weight. Neither test return nor test risk metrics enter selection. Only the three validation-selected candidates are reported on the final test block.

All PAA evaluations use deterministic policy actions, meaning that the policy chooses its predicted mean action rather than sampling an action, and a cash-only start. The AR(1) random-signal control PAA additionally uses a reproducible noise realization, with separate validation and test seeds. A seed fixes the random-number sequence used in a run. Validation blocks contain 289 or 290 recorded dates. The test block contains 252 dates and 251 transitions. Returns \(R_{k,b}\) are net of simulated transaction costs and include daily cash return \(q_t\) from Equation~\eqref{eq:cash-rate}. The hierarchical PAA candidates retain all 26 market indicators in each asset token and use the same frozen SAA run \texttt{00312}. Their configured training budget is 18 million transitions.

Reported PAA Sharpe ratio \(\mathrm{SR}=\sqrt{252}\,\bar r/s(r)\) uses mean daily simple return \(\bar r\) and sample standard deviation \(s(r)\), excluding the initialization row. It measures return relative to daily variability without subtracting the cash rate \(q_t\). Maximum drawdown \(M\) is reported as a positive loss magnitude in the PAA tables. Net asset value \(V_t\), abbreviated NAV, is cash plus current holdings. Executed-notional turnover \(T=\sum_t n_t^{\mathrm{traded}}/V_t\) sums absolute daily traded notional \(n_t^{\mathrm{traded}}\) relative to NAV \(V_t\). Turnover \(T=40\) means repeated trading totaling forty times changing NAV, not a \(4000\%\) loss. Risky exposure \(e_t=1-w_t^{\mathrm{live},(0)}\) is the share invested in non-cash assets, including commodities. Transaction-cost share \(c^{\mathrm{tc}}\) divides cumulative charged cost by initial capital \(V_0\). Means of block-level returns \(R_{k,b}\), Sharpe ratios \(\mathrm{SR}_b\), and drawdowns \(M_b\) summarize independently reset blocks, not one concatenated path.

\subsubsection{Discussion}
\label{sec:paa-results-discussion}

\paragraph{Feature engineering and simulator design.}
The extensive feature engineering and simulator design are substantial methodological contributions of this thesis. The shared pipeline combines multi-horizon returns, volatility, volume, cross-asset relationships, fractional-price feature \(\phi_t\), calendar vector \(\mathbf g_t\), and dynamic cash-rate inputs \(z_t^{\mathrm{rf}},q_t\). These provide comparable representations across eleven assets while preserving different forms of historical context. Their integration with transaction costs, account statistics, and isolated SAA accounts creates a reusable experimental framework for training, interpreting, and comparing the SAA and PAA variants.

\paragraph{Allocation success is not the same as successful signal transfer.}
The hierarchical PAA, AR(1) random-signal control PAA, and cross-sectional-only PAA all show positive SPY-relative validation means \(J_{\mathrm{val}}\) and test excess \(J_{\mathrm{test}}\). Allocation can exploit cross-asset opportunities rather than merely predict one asset's next return. Changing weights \(w_t^{\mathrm{post},(i)}\) in Figures~\ref{fig:paa-hier-test}, \ref{fig:paa-ar1-test}, and~\ref{fig:paa-cross-test} illustrate this distinction. Each PAA chooses both risky exposure \(e_t\) and its composition, subject to costs and account state (Section~\ref{sec:paa-action}). These outcomes do not establish that attention is superior to another architecture, because attention itself is not separately ablated.

The selected hierarchical PAA exceeds the AR(1) random-signal control PAA by \(2.86\) points in mean validation return and \(28.67\) points on test. It exceeds the cross-sectional-only PAA by \(0.42\) and \(23.47\) points. These selected-run comparisons support a positive association between transferred SAA information and cumulative return \(R\), especially on test. They do not establish a general advantage across runs. Hierarchical PAA family mean excess \(\overline J_{\mathrm{val}}=3.31\) points remains below the cross-sectional-only PAA's \(4.44\) points, and the selected hierarchical PAA incurs greater drawdown \(M\) and cost \(c^{\mathrm{tc}}\).

The cross-sectional-only PAA result does not imply that temporal information is irrelevant. Multi-horizon returns, fractional-price feature \(\phi_t\), recorded targets \(w_{t-1}^{(i)}\), and running account statistics already summarize history (Sections~\ref{sec:state-returns}, \ref{sec:state-ffd}, and~\ref{sec:paa-state}). The hierarchical PAA transfers request \(a_t^{(i)}\), exposure \(\rho_t^{(i)}\), and risk summaries \(S_t^{\mathrm{sh},(i)},M_t^{\mathrm{sh},(i)}\) together. Replacing the action source also changes shadow-account histories. The cross-sectional-only PAA retains risk-summary fields for untraded cash accounts. The comparison tests this combined interface, not an isolated action-only effect. The recurrent-memory motivation \cite{hausknecht2015deep} is therefore conditional on what historical information other inputs already supply.

\paragraph{Regime correspondence between the SAA and hierarchical PAA.}
The two layers do not share a simple block ranking. SAA mean asset alpha \(\overline\alpha_{02}^{\mathrm{asset}}=4.58\) points in val\_02 accompanies the hierarchical PAA's strongest validation return \(R_{02}=44.84\%\). The hierarchical PAA leads the cross-sectional-only PAA by \(32.59\) points in that block, but trails it by \(10.97\) points in val\_03. SAA mean asset alpha \(\overline\alpha_{03}^{\mathrm{asset}}=1.16\) points is still positive in val\_03. Positive isolated timing performance therefore does not ensure beneficial allocation.

The market conditions in Table~\ref{tab:oos-blocks} help interpret the differences. Val\_02 contains a broad decline followed by recovery. The SAA equal-weight portfolio falls materially and recovers only to \(R_{\mathrm{SAA},02}=1.41\%\) (Figure~\ref{fig:saa-agg-val02}). The hierarchical PAA instead makes large early gains, loses part of them, and advances strongly late (Figure~\ref{fig:paa-hier-val02}). Its \(43.42\)-point advantage over SAA equal-weight is associated with cross-asset allocation, not replication of isolated SAA accounts. Composition changes are consistent with useful timing context combined with active selection, but the graph alone cannot establish which input caused a trade.

Val\_03 contains a concentration-led rally and two distinct shocks. The SAA lags Gold and EWG, but eight SAA accounts beat their own references and aggregate return \(R_{\mathrm{SAA},03}=11.14\%\) exceeds EW. Hierarchical PAA return \(R_{03}=5.31\%\) is lower despite average risky exposure \(\bar e_{03}=95.73\%\). Figure~\ref{fig:paa-hier-val03} shows repeated concentration and a late reversal rather than excessive cash holdings. The cross-sectional-only PAA achieves higher return \(R_{03}=16.28\%\) and shallower drawdown \(M_{03}=9.62\%\) through its own rotations (Figure~\ref{fig:paa-cross-val03}). This block shows that positive SAA account performance need not be used profitably by the hierarchical PAA.

The other blocks reinforce this distinction. Val\_00 has the SAA's highest mean asset alpha \(\overline\alpha_{00}^{\mathrm{asset}}=8.66\) points and aggregate return \(R_{\mathrm{SAA},00}=34.87\%\), yet hierarchical PAA return \(R_{00}=8.78\%\) trails both PAA ablations and passive market references. In val\_01, negative SAA mean asset alpha \(\overline\alpha_{01}^{\mathrm{asset}}=-1.07\) points coexists with the hierarchical PAA leading all three selected PAAs. SAA alpha \(\alpha_i^{\mathrm{asset}}\) compares each instrument with its own reference, whereas PAA excess \(J_{\mathrm{val}}\) compares a multi-asset account with SPY. The SAA supplies a position-change request \(a_t^{(i)}\), not a calibrated ranking of expected cross-asset returns.

\paragraph{What the shadow portfolios establish.}
Recorded consistency checks for hierarchical PAA run \texttt{00317} show no missing shadow-action commits, no non-finite account values, and zero discrepancy between injected and independently regenerated SAA requests \(a_t^{(i)}\) and shadow values \(U_t^{(i)}\) in all five blocks. Maximum weight-sum error is below \(7.4\times10^{-8}\). This supports the interface's accounting and signal-transfer consistency (Section~\ref{sec:info-flow}). Isolation prevents hierarchical PAA trades from rewriting SAA account state. It does not improve the SAA policy by itself.

The standalone SAA and hierarchical PAA-harness SAA reference also agree on validation cumulative returns within \(0.00002\) percentage points. Both yield approximately \(34.87\%\), \(12.08\%\), \(1.41\%\), and \(11.14\%\) across the four blocks. Isolated accounts use SAA training capital and frictions. Balances and charged costs are scaled to reporting capital before averaging, not re-executed at a larger account size. This agreement connects Figures~\ref{fig:saa-agg-val02} and~\ref{fig:saa-agg-val03} with the SAA references in Figures~\ref{fig:paa-hier-val02} and~\ref{fig:paa-hier-val03}. It does not decompose hierarchical PAA return \(R_b\) into a causal SAA contribution.

Transferred request \(a_t^{(i)}\), holding fraction \(\rho_t^{(i)}\), Sortino \(S_t^{\mathrm{sh},(i)}\), and drawdown \(M_t^{\mathrm{sh},(i)}\) are features rather than instructions (Section~\ref{sec:paa-state}). They exclude recurrent hidden state \(h_t^{(i)}\) and calibrated forecast confidence. Shadow Sortino \(S_t^{\mathrm{sh},(i)}\) uses net percentage account return, including positive cash carry and charged costs. Its EMA smoothing, \(0.005\) downside-deviation floor, and clipping to \([-3,3]\) distinguish it from exported financial ratios. Both actor and critic use contextualized tokens, but PPO is not guaranteed to learn which input to discount. The AR(1) random-signal control PAA's strong val\_03 result does not establish that random signals predict returns. Other retained inputs can support profitable allocation.

\paragraph{Return, risk, and the reward counterfactual.}
SPY as reward counterfactual \(B_t\) (Section~\ref{sec:excess-return}) makes SPY-relative excess an explicit objective, not a guarantee of diversified risk-adjusted superiority. On test, the hierarchical PAA beats SPY, EW, and SAA equal-weight, but drawdown \(M_{\mathrm{test}}=31.33\%\) is approximately \(3.43\) times SPY's (Figure~\ref{fig:paa-hier-test}). The AR(1) random-signal control PAA trails EW (Figure~\ref{fig:paa-ar1-test}). The cross-sectional-only PAA nearly matches EW with slightly smaller drawdown \(M_{\mathrm{test}}=9.51\%\), but lower Sharpe ratio \(\mathrm{SR}_{\mathrm{test}}=1.52\) (Figure~\ref{fig:paa-cross-test}). The hierarchical PAA's highest terminal return \(R_{\mathrm{test}}\) therefore does not imply the best outcome for every risk preference.

Composition panels show cross-sectional rotation, the role assigned to attention in Section~\ref{sec:self-attention}. Hierarchical PAA average exposure \(\bar e_b\) is approximately \(96\%\), while PAA ablation exposures are approximately \(92\%\). Commodity and equity weights change markedly in Figures~\ref{fig:paa-hier-val02}, \ref{fig:paa-hier-val03}, and~\ref{fig:paa-cross-test}. Fixed cash logit \(z_t^{(0)}=0\) does not fix cash weight \(w_t^{(0)}\). Remaining invested is an observed policy outcome. Exposure \(e_t\) is not a complete risk measure. Replacing equities with Crude can change concentration and volatility without changing total exposure \(e_t\). Grey bars are approximately exposure-neutral, not risk-neutral. The plots demonstrate active selection, not uniformly successful diversification.

The drawdown-aware reward \(\mathcal R_t\) needs a configuration-specific reading. The selected hierarchical PAA uses zero sustained-drawdown coefficient \(\lambda_{dd}^{\mathrm{lvl}}\). Execution gap \(G_t^{\mathrm{paa}}\) is diagnostic rather than a reward subtraction (Section~\ref{sec:reward}). Active incremental drawdown \(\Delta\mathrm{DD}_t^+\) and differential Sortino \(\Delta S_t\) compete with SPY-relative log return \(\Delta\ell_t\). They do not impose a hard drawdown limit. The late test decline in Figure~\ref{fig:paa-hier-test} illustrates this distinction. Hybrid-policy studies motivate combining temporal representation and allocation, but use their own data, trading frequencies, and risk settings \cite{JunKevin.2025,GabrielNixonRaj.2025}. Their gains do not establish superiority of this frozen interface under the present objective.

Net returns \(R_b\) already absorb configured friction, but turnover \(T_b\) of tens of times NAV makes economic value sensitive to commission, spread, and impact assumptions (Section~\ref{sec:friction}). In val\_03, the hierarchical PAA trades more than the cross-sectional-only PAA while earning less. On test, the hierarchical PAA has both the greatest turnover \(T_{\mathrm{test}}=51.86\) and the highest return \(R_{\mathrm{test}}=55.30\%\). Trading intensity is therefore not a reliable proxy for quality (Figures~\ref{fig:paa-hier-val03}, \ref{fig:paa-cross-val03}, and~\ref{fig:paa-hier-test}). Lower cross-sectional-only PAA cost \(c_{\mathrm{test}}^{\mathrm{tc}}=0.67\%\) remains practically relevant, but no result establishes that simulated frictions capture every live-trading cost.

\paragraph{Checkpoint stability and seed dependence.}
The retained hierarchical PAA run \texttt{00317} checkpoint occurs at \(16.83\) million transitions, unlike the early selected SAA run \texttt{00312} checkpoint at \(0.40\) million. The different positions in Figures~\ref{fig:excess_return_over_training} and~\ref{fig:saa_train_vs_validation} show why one training duration is not appropriate for both layers. The SAA graph directly shows training excess \(D_{\mathrm{train}}^{\mathrm{BH}}\) rising while later validation excess \(D_{\mathrm{val}}^{\mathrm{BH}}\) weakens. Figure~\ref{fig:excess_return_over_training} additionally shows sustained positive validation excess \(D_{\mathrm{val}}^{\mathrm{SPY}}\) after approximately ten million transitions. This supports within-run persistence beyond one isolated favorable checkpoint, although the validation observations are not independent replications.

Hierarchical PAA mean validation excess \(J_{\mathrm{val}}\) ranges from \(0.36\) to \(8.43\) points across the four candidates. This large spread reflects run dependence, including seeds and observed training durations. Checkpoints within a run share training and validation data and are not independent replications. Neither the best checkpoint nor an apparently stable curve establishes a universal SAA contribution without replication and an appropriate statistical comparison.

\paragraph{Generalization and strength of evidence.}
Test returns \(R_{\mathrm{test}}\) and Sharpe ratios \(\mathrm{SR}_{\mathrm{test}}\) exceed each selected PAA's validation mean (Table~\ref{tab:paa-validation-test}). Hierarchical PAA SPY excess rises from \(J_{\mathrm{val}}=8.43\) to \(J_{\mathrm{test}}=33.08\) points, but drawdown rises from \(\overline M_{\mathrm{val}}=18.01\%\) to \(M_{\mathrm{test}}=31.33\%\). AR(1) random-signal control PAA excess falls from \(5.56\) to \(4.40\) points and its drawdown also increases. Cross-sectional-only PAA excess rises from \(8.01\) to \(9.61\) points while drawdown decreases. Stronger passive test returns and different horizons prevent raw return increases from establishing general robustness. The direct observation is positive SPY-relative net return in one reserved block, with different risk costs.

Validation-only policy selection preserves the separation in Section~\ref{sec:oos-protocol}, but maximizing across checkpoints and configurations can inflate validation performance \cite{LopezdePrado.2018}. Hierarchical PAA validation excess \(J_{\mathrm{val}}\) ranges from \(0.36\) to \(8.43\) points, while cross-sectional-only PAA excess ranges from \(-0.06\) to \(8.01\). The selected-model validation gap is only \(0.42\) points. Selecting the best candidate separately for each family is not a paired statistical test. Different selected seeds, unequal training durations, one AR(1) realization per evaluation, and one held-out block limit causal conclusions. The large hierarchical PAA test advantage and small validation margin require separate interpretation.

Overall, the evidence supports a functioning SAA-to-hierarchical-PAA interface and profitable SPY-relative allocation. Selected hierarchical PAA run \texttt{00317} has the highest mean validation return and test return among the selected PAAs. Its advantage is especially large in val\_02 and test\_00, but accompanies greater turnover, cost, and test drawdown. The cross-sectional-only PAA retains the higher family-average validation excess and lower selected-model drawdowns. The thesis therefore distinguishes successful implementation, profitable allocation, and a general causal benefit from transferred SAA information. The first two are supported. The selected-run comparison supports the third only conditionally, not as a universal result across runs and regimes.
