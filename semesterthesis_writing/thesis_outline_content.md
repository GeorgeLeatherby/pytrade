# Semesterthesis: Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning for Multi-Asset Trading

**Topic:** Separating single asset temporal signal extraction from multi-asset attention for dynamic allocation in DRL for portfolio allocation problems \
**Environment:** Custom coded env called PyTrade

## 1. Introduction

* **1.1 Motivation:** Financial markets are a challenging domain for AI research: high volatility, low signal-to-noise ratio, non-stationarity of raw data, and complex sequential decision-making under uncertainty. This thesis does not aim to generate novel alpha or outperform established benchmarks; it investigates whether decomposing portfolio allocation into specialized modules improves learning stability and efficiency. Motivating proposition: passive buy-and-hold on broad-market ETFs may be inferior to active strategies that use end-of-day price signals to capture trends and manage downside risk (Faber; Moskowitz). Extracting stationary, memory-preserving trading signals from non-stationary data while simultaneously determining optimal portfolio weights is difficult; static allocation or purely predictive forecasting fail to adapt to regime changes or capture the sequential nature of trading decisions. DRL is well-suited to this transition from static forecasting to sequential decision-making, since it naturally models sequential decisions, handles continuous action spaces, and learns policies that generalize beyond training data. Two fundamental challenges are named explicitly: (1) the action space is high-dimensional and continuous, since the agent must size allocations across multiple assets simultaneously, and (2) financial data has an extremely low signal-to-noise ratio, with genuine economic signal obscured by microstructure noise and random-walk dynamics. The research question, stated directly: *Does modularizing temporal (single-asset) and cross-sectional (portfolio-level) functions improve the stability and efficiency of deep reinforcement learning agents for dynamic portfolio allocation?*
* **1.2 Problem Description:** Hierarchical decomposition as the proposed answer: Layer 1 (Single-Asset Agent, SAA) extracts temporal features from individual assets using recurrent neural networks, learning asset-specific temporal dynamics in isolation; Layer 2 (Portfolio Allocator Agent, PAA) uses these pre-extracted signals alongside raw market data to determine optimal portfolio weights via self-attention, so each module can specialize to its respective task. The architecture is evaluated through walk-forward, out-of-sample testing and multiple baseline comparisons to distinguish genuine alpha from memorized patterns, isolating the contribution of each module as empirical evidence for (or against) hierarchical decomposition as a viable paradigm for financial RL.

## 2. Literature Review

* **2.1 Sequential Decision-Making and Continuous Control in Finance:** RL models sequential decision-making under uncertainty as an MDP; deep neural networks as high-capacity function approximators for policy networks (Goodfellow et al.). PPO (Schulman et al.) resolves the high variance and sample inefficiency of traditional policy gradients in continuous action domains via a clipped surrogate objective that bounds policy-ratio updates. In quant finance, portfolio management is a continuous control problem; early frameworks discretized decisions into buy/sell/hold, formalized by López de Prado's triple-barrier method, but discrete actions cannot dynamically size allocations across a multi-asset portfolio. PPO in continuous action spaces for dynamic portfolio management, using shared representation layers split into actor/critic heads (Wu et al.); FinRL (Liu et al.) as a standardized benchmark environment/library; behavioral-finance-constrained continuous policy optimization embedding loss aversion and overconfidence into the reward (Charkhestani & Esfahanipour).
* **2.2 Sequential Representation and Temporal Memory in Non-Stationary Markets:** Financial markets are non-stationary, partially observable systems where observable price feeds do not capture full macroeconomic state or private order flow, breaking the standard Markov assumption. Deep Recurrent Q-Learning (Hausknecht & Stone) as the POMDP-handling precedent via recurrent memory layers; LSTM (Hochreiter & Schmidhuber) gating mechanisms (input/forget/output gates) eliminating vanishing gradients and capturing long-range dependencies. Applying sequential models to financial data trades off stationarity against memory: standard integer differencing (log returns) achieves stationarity but erases persistent price levels and macro trends; fractional differentiation (López de Prado) resolves this by applying non-integer differencing operators that satisfy stationarity while preserving maximum historical memory. Adjacent sequence architectures: Temporal Fusion Transformers combining recurrence with temporal self-attention for interpretable multi-horizon forecasting (Lim et al.); a broader evaluation of Transformer capacity for long-term dependency modeling (Shen et al.); despite these advances, recurrent state representations remain the primary choice for low-latency online continuous control.
* **2.3 Self-Attention and Cross-Sectional Asset Interaction:** Attention mechanisms compute dynamic, context-relative weightings across input features; the Transformer (Vaswani et al.) replaces recurrent sequence bottlenecks with multi-head self-attention via Query/Key/Value pairwise interactions across a sequence or set. A survey of Transformer-based RL documenting attention's handling of multi-modal context, trajectory history, and spatial representation (Hu et al.); Transformer encoders improving representation learning for online continuous control (Kachaev et al.). In multi-asset portfolio management, risk and return cannot be evaluated per asset in isolation; traditional empirical covariance matrices degrade during regime shifts, while attention can learn non-linear cross-asset dependencies directly from data: attention-enhanced RL for dynamic portfolio optimization modeling transaction-cost frictions and multi-asset risk metrics (Xue & Ye); neural portfolio allocators processing distinct asset representations via multi-head attention (Coulter).
* **2.4 Modular Decomposition and Hierarchical Policy Architectures:** Monolithic DRL agents risk policy collapse when forced to jointly solve temporal feature extraction, cross-sectional covariance modeling, and portfolio constraint satisfaction (Hu et al.; Kevin & Yugopuspito). Hierarchical RL (HRL) mitigates this by decomposing tasks into specialized, modular sub-policies: a hybrid LSTM-PPO framework decoupling recurrent temporal features from policy optimization for dynamic portfolio optimization, improving learning stability (Kevin & Yugopuspito); adaptive, regime-aware RL emphasizing specialization across volatility regimes for robustness (Raj); distinct term structure, volatility skew, and cross-correlation patterns across asset classes motivating localized risk management (Bennett). Gap identified: combining single-asset temporal feature extraction with multi-asset attention mechanisms within one unified hierarchical DRL framework remains underexplored — the gap PyTrade addresses by decoupling single-asset temporal signals from portfolio-level allocation.

## 3. System Design and Methodology

General framing: PyTrade separates two problems usually learned jointly — extracting a temporal trading signal from a single instrument, and allocating capital across many instruments — and trains/evaluates them as two distinct layers rather than one monolithic policy.

* **3.1 System Overview:** SAA (Layer 1) is a recurrent policy trading one instrument in isolation from its own price history and current position; PAA (Layer 2) is a self-attention policy observing the whole 11-asset universe at once, consuming the SAA's per-asset signal as one input among others and learning how much to trust, discount, or override it per asset. Shared environment vocabulary: discrete daily steps, observation → action → reward cycle, 252-step (~1 year) episodes sampled from a contiguous historical block, PPO updates after each batch; historical prices are exogenous, replayed unchanged (deterministic transitions given the data and the agent's own action; no market-impact-driven price feedback). Rationale for the split: the temporal problem is a sequential-memory problem well matched to an LSTM, while the cross-sectional problem is a permutation-invariant set problem well matched to self-attention; the architecture builds this decomposition in directly rather than asking one network to discover it from reward alone. Formal shared framework: a POMDP tuple $\mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},\mathcal{O},\mathcal{P},\mathcal{R},\mathcal{Z})$, instantiated separately for each agent in 3.4/3.5; both layers retain PPO's discount factor $\gamma$ and GAE's decay parameter $\lambda$ as a deliberate variance-reduction choice rather than exploiting the finite horizon to set $\gamma=1$. Partial observability has three distinct sources: (1) the dominant, irreducible unobservability of the true market-generating process, (2) the deliberate engineering choice to reduce each asset to a small, stationary technical-feature panel, and (3) the SAA's LSTM cell state, which is never re-exposed to the environment/reward and is the only source the POMDP formalism actually targets; the PAA's own policy, by contrast, is a memoryless function of its current observation, with any memory arriving already compressed through the frozen SAA's signal.
* **3.2 State Representation: A Shared Feature Engineering Pipeline:** Raw OHLCV is non-stationary and stacking more raw history does not fix that; all market features are produced once, offline, by a `DataEnricher` pipeline and cached by `MarketDataCache`, never computed at runtime (portfolio features like cash/weights/costs are computed per step at runtime instead). Two transforms recur throughout: the log return over $N$ days, and rolling $z$-score standardization over a shared window $w_{\text{norm}}=36$ trading days.
    * **3.2.1 Multi-Horizon Return, Volume, and Volatility Features:** computed at horizons $N\in\{1,5,21\}$; SPY-relative log return, $z$-scored (`z_rel_log_close`), deliberately market-relative so a broad rally/sell-off is not read as asset-specific signal; a volatility-breakout companion (`z_vol_breakout`); an analogous market-relative volume-intensity pair (`log_volume_intensity`, `norm_volume_feature`).
    * **3.2.2 Intraday Shape and Classical Technical Indicators:** per-day OHLC bar-shape features (intraday range, closing strength, overnight gap, upper-shadow ratio) and their normalized forms; three classical indicators rescaled to be dimensionless and scale-free — RSI(14) (`rsi_14`), Bollinger Band width relative to price (`bb_width_reworked`), and Average True Range (`atr_z_norm`).
    * **3.2.3 Cross-Asset Features:** rolling 21-day Fisher-$z$-transformed correlation to SPY (`z_fisher_corr_spy`), rolling 36-day beta vs. SPY (`z_beta_spy`), and cross-sectional return dispersion across the universe (`z_market_dispersion`), giving the PAA's attention genuine correlation structure to work with.
    * **3.2.4 Memory-Preserving Stationary Features via Fractional Differentiation:** the binomial-series fractional-differencing operator $(1-L)^d$ for non-integer $d$, trading off stationarity against long-range memory better than integer differencing (López de Prado); fixed-width weight truncation; a "golden-$d$" search (smallest $d\in[0.45,0.8]$ passing an ADF stationarity test) run independently for three anchor assets (SPY, Gold, Crude), with $d^\ast=\max$ across anchors applied uniformly to all 11 assets; a closing per-asset ADF verification gate requiring a 100% pass rate; produces `norm_ffd_feature`, the only price-level (as opposed to return/ratio/oscillator) feature in the pipeline.
    * **3.2.5 Calendar Encoding:** six $(\sin,\cos)$ pair features for day-of-week (5-cycle), day-of-month (trading-day rank), and month-of-year (12-cycle), avoiding artificial discontinuities at period boundaries. Feature selection into the SAA, PAA asset tokens, and PAA portfolio token is entirely config-driven per experiment.
* **3.3 A Shared Reward Design Methodology:** both layers share one reward methodology applied to two notions of "portfolio" (the SAA's private sub-portfolio, the PAA's live universe); the design goal is dense, per-step reward of risk-adjusted skill rather than market beta exposure.
    * **3.3.1 Multi-Objective Reward Composition:** five raw components — excess return, risk-adjusted trend (differential Sortino), drawdown shock, sustained drawdown, execution gap — summed, then $\tanh$-squashed and rescaled so no single component or badly-scaled coefficient dominates the gradient.
    * **3.3.2 Excess Return over a Passive Counterfactual:** both agents are rewarded on log excess return over a passive counterfactual (SAA: same-asset buy-and-hold; PAA: SPY buy-and-hold) rather than raw return, isolating timing/selection skill from beta exposure to a rising market.
    * **3.3.3 Risk-Adjusted Trend — the Differential Sortino Ratio:** EMA-tracked mean return and downside variance feed a Sortino ratio (like Sharpe, but penalizing only downside deviation); the reward term is the step-wise *change* in this ratio, giving a dense signal without waiting for episode termination.
    * **3.3.4 Drawdown Penalties:** an incremental penalty on newly incurred drawdown (over a growing lookback window) plus a persistent penalty for every step spent underwater relative to the running peak.
    * **3.3.5 The Execution Gap:** penalizes the distance between requested and actually-filled action/weight-delta beyond a deadband, with an asymmetric multiplier, without punishing trades the environment declined to fill for lack of cash/inventory.
* **3.4 Layer 1: The Single-Asset Agent (SAA):** the temporal specialist; one randomly selected asset per episode; the only recurrent policy in the thesis (2-layer LSTM).
    * **3.4.1 State, Observation, and Action Space:** state = (close price, cash, held shares); observation = 32 market/calendar features + a 6-entry portfolio memory block (log cash ratio, log notional ratio, previous sub-portfolio return, previous action, risk-free rate $z$-score, risk-free excess return) + an 11-dim one-hot asset id; action = a single continuous scalar in $[-1,1]$ interpreted as a target position *change*, scaled by an annealed action-limiting factor $\kappa_t$.
    * **3.4.2 Network Architecture:** `InputMLPFeatures` embeds the one-hot asset id (11→6) separately from the raw market/memory block before concatenation; MLP (128→64) into a 2-layer LSTM (hidden size 64); independent linear actor and critic heads reading the same LSTM hidden state.
    * **3.4.3 Why Actor and Critic Outputs Are Never Recombined:** PPO's clipped surrogate depends only on the policy probability ratio; the critic is a variance-reduction baseline for GAE, not a candidate action component — recombining the two heads would inject the critic's regression noise into a real trade for the SAA, and is a mathematical no-op for the PAA (softmax shift-invariance, Section 3.5.5).
    * **3.4.4 Dual-Recursion Logic — LSTM Memory and Generalized Advantage Estimation:** the LSTM's hidden/cell state gives within-episode memory that already informs the critic's value estimate during rollout collection; GAE itself runs unmodified over the full, un-truncated rollout; only the PPO optimization phase truncates backpropagation-through-time to per-episode sequences.
    * **3.4.5 Training Dynamics and Stability:** three-phase (hold/ramp/hold) linear schedules for learning rate, entropy coefficient, clip range, and $\kappa_t$, tuned against early policy collapse (long-only/short-only) and oscillatory high-turnover behavior; a structural transfer risk remains if the frozen SAA's recurrent state were ever updated from a live, PAA-controlled portfolio it never saw during training — resolved by the shadow-portfolio design (Section 3.5.2).
* **3.5 Layer 2: The Portfolio Allocator Agent (PAA):** the cross-sectional specialist; consumes one frozen SAA copy per asset as a feature rather than learning its own timing opinion from scratch.
    * **3.5.1 State Space — Tokenizing the Asset and Portfolio Universe:** observation = $N{+}1$ tokens ($N$ asset tokens + 1 portfolio token), each linearly embedded to a shared $d_{\text{model}}=64$; asset token (29 raw dims) = 26 market features (the SAA's own features minus calendar) + SAA signal $a_t^{(i)}$ + shadow holding % $\rho_t^{(i)}$ + live PAA weight $w_t^{(i)}$; portfolio token (32 raw dims) = 6 calendar features + 12 live weights (cash + 11 assets) + 14 running portfolio-risk metrics (excess return, drawdown, Sortino/EMA state, risk-free terms, Sharpe, volatility, previous max drawdown, turnover, concentration, raw risk-free rate). The PAA recomputes its observation from scratch every step and holds no hidden state of its own — a memoryless policy matching the Markov assumption of standard, non-recurrent PPO.
    * **3.5.2 Integrating the SAA — Shadow Portfolios:** one frozen SAA policy is evaluated once per step in a single batched forward pass, one row (and one indexed LSTM hidden state) per asset; each row owns a private, hypothetical shadow sub-portfolio that mirrors what the SAA alone would have done since episode start, and the SAA's own observation is rebuilt only from that shadow book, never the live portfolio — by construction, the PAA's own trades can never perturb any SAA copy's recurrent state, avoiding the domain-shift risk of Section 3.4.5. The shadow-executed action ($a_t^{(i)}$) and the shadow book's own holding percentage ($\rho_t^{(i)}$) are the two signals injected into the asset token; the live PAA weight $w_t^{(i)}$ is read separately from the real environment state.
    * **3.5.3 Action Space — Softmax Allocation with a Fixed Cash Anchor:** actor outputs $N$ real-valued logits; a cash logit fixed at 0 is prepended (removing a redundant degree of freedom under softmax's shift-invariance) before a stable softmax onto the simplex; a soft-execution step moves the live portfolio only part-way toward the target weights, with a deadband suppressing sub-cost-threshold trades; reward is measured against a 100%-invested, transaction-cost-paying SPY buy-and-hold, distinct from two diagnostic-only portfolios (a static comparison clone and a fixed-weight industry benchmark) that never enter the reward.
    * **3.5.4 Network Architecture — Shared Attention with Private Heads:** a `SAATokenizer` embeds each token type to $d_{\text{model}}$ and adds a gated, learned asset-id embedding plus LayerNorm; a shared 2-layer `TransformerEncoder` (4 heads, Pre-LN, GELU) attends across all $N{+}1$ tokens; the architecture then splits into private single-layer encoders — the actor branch sees only the (mean-pooled) asset tokens, the critic branch sees only the portfolio token — before the final actor head (logits + cash anchor → softmax) and critic head (scalar $V(s_t)$).
    * **3.5.5 Actor-Critic Interaction:** no direct connection between the two heads (adding the critic's scalar to every asset logit before softmax would be a mathematical no-op, since softmax is shift-invariant); the two heads interact only indirectly, through gradients from both losses flowing into the shared trunk, and through the critic's value estimate shaping the advantage used in the policy loss; Xavier initialization with GPT-2-style residual scaling stabilizes the shared trunk.
* **3.6 Simulator Design:** a custom Gymnasium-compliant `TradingEnv` (standard `reset()`/`step()` interface, framework-agnostic NumPy I/O), built from scratch because off-the-shelf frameworks (e.g. FinRL's fixed continuous-weight, single-policy design) do not natively support mode-switching, shadow states, and frozen-sub-policy multi-agent interaction under one shared feature cache and cost model.
    * **3.6.1 Execution Modes and Data Infrastructure:** four config-selected modes (SAA target-position, PAA portfolio-weights, and two instruction-list modes used to verify execution logic against rule-based strategies); `MarketDataCache` precomputes/memory-maps the full feature panel once per run; `EpisodeBuffer` records rolling step history for the reward and observation functions to read back without recomputation.
    * **3.6.2 Purged, Block-Level Train/Validation/Test Splitting:** the split is built once on the continuous date range as repeated purged train/validation cycles (target 20% validation share per super-block) separated by a fixed 60-trading-day purge gap sized against the pipeline's rolling feature windows (up to 36/120 days), followed by one held-out test block touched exactly once; episodes are sampled at the block level, never straddling a purge gap or block boundary; no purge is needed on the validation→training edge since only training-into-evaluation leakage matters. This is a practical, purged walk-forward compromise in place of full Combinatorial Purged Cross-Validation (López de Prado), which would be computationally intractable to repeat across many PPO training runs.
    * **3.6.3 Hardware Placement — CPU Simulation, GPU Inference:** environment logic (execution, cost model, reward bookkeeping) is scalar and branch-heavy and stays on CPU `SubprocVecEnv` workers; every neural forward/backward pass — the frozen SAA batched across $k\times N$ asset rows, the PAA's `AttentionEngine` batched across $k$ environments — is batched into large, regular GPU tensor operations before control returns to the CPU-side environments.
* **3.7 Friction Modeling:** every trade pays commission + half-spread (both linear in traded notional) + a market-impact term scaling with the square root of trade size relative to trailing average daily volume (consistent with sublinear empirical microstructure impact) + an optional fixed per-order fee; all coefficients are independently configurable per experiment, and setting them to zero recovers a frictionless environment for isolating signal quality from realized net returns.
* **3.8 Asset Universe:** 11 liquid, USD-denominated, US-exchange-listed instruments spanning equities, a commodity complex, and precious metals — SPY, seven country ETFs (EWG, EWH, EWJ, EWQ, EWS, EWT, EWU, EWY), Crude, and Gold — chosen to remove currency risk as a confound (an FX-conversion step exists in the pipeline but is a documented no-op for this universe), to avoid single-name/corporate-action noise (ETFs/futures rather than single stocks), and for cross-sectional diversity across regions and asset classes rather than any hand-picked pairwise relationship, so the PAA's attention mechanism must discover regime-dependent co-movement rather than have it engineered into the reward or features.

## 4. Experimental Validation & Testing

General framing: before any result is shown, this chapter fixes what is being compared and why, mirroring Section 3's approach of explaining mechanism before evidence. Every comparison in this chapter follows directly from a claim made in Section 3: the excess-return reward design (3.3.2) is only meaningful if the agent is actually shown to beat the passive counterfactual it is scored against, not just a strawman; the shadow-portfolio/frozen-SAA-signal design (3.5.2) is only justified if removing or corrupting that signal measurably hurts the PAA; and the modular decomposition itself (3.1) is only a valid answer to the thesis's research question if the two-layer system outperforms both a signal-free cross-sectional model and, ideally, a monolithic single-network alternative. Baselines are therefore chosen to isolate one specific claim each, not to maximize the number of comparison lines on a chart, and the purged, block-level OOS protocol (3.6.2) is what makes any of these comparisons trustworthy rather than an artifact of in-sample memorization.

* **4.1 Out-of-Sample (OOS) Protocol:** recap of the purged, block-level splitting scheme (3.6.2): repeated purged train/validation cycles yield four validation blocks spanning distinct historical regimes (used iteratively for checkpoint selection and the diagnostics below), plus one held-out test block, preceded by its own purge gap, that is touched exactly once for the final reported result. This distinction, iterative validation vs. single-shot test, is what separates "peak alpha" checkpoint selection (4.2.5) from an inflated, cherry-picked headline number.

* **4.2 SAA Results:**
    * **4.2.1 Baselines Compared:** three lines per chart — 100% cash, 100% buy-and-hold (same asset), and the SAA agent. The previously planned 50% cash/invested baseline is dropped: it sits uninformatively between the two extremes and adds no distinct claim beyond what cash-only and buy-and-hold already bound.
    * **4.2.2 Per-Asset, Per-Validation-Episode Detail:** already computed — one equity-curve chart per asset for each of the 4 validation episodes (cash vs. buy-and-hold vs. agent), read for whether the agent tracks or beats buy-and-hold specifically during trending vs. choppy sub-periods, and whether it visibly avoids the worst of buy-and-hold's drawdowns.
    * **4.2.3 Aggregated Cross-Asset View vs. SPY:** already computed — for each validation episode, all SAA candidates' results aggregated and compared against SPY. Central empirical finding to report and interpret: individual outperformance vs. SPY is high-variance (some asset/episode combinations negative, some strongly positive), yet the combined, cross-asset average outperformance is positive in all 4 validation episodes and exceeds +1% vs. SPY on average. This is the chapter's key argument for the SAA: framed not as a claim of large, reliable per-asset alpha, but as evidence of a small, non-degenerate, and consistently positive timing signal, which is precisely the property that justifies feeding it into the PAA (3.5.2) rather than discarding it as noise.
    * **4.2.4 Training Diagnostics (TensorBoard):** selected to substantiate, not just illustrate, the training-stability claims of Section 3.4.5. Planned graphs: (i) entropy coefficient schedule vs. realized policy entropy, evidence for or against premature collapse into a long-only/short-only trap; (ii) the reward component breakdown over training steps (excess return, Sortino term, drawdown penalties, execution gap), showing which term dominates early vs. late in training; (iii) train-episode reward vs. validation-episode reward across checkpoints, the direct evidence for validation decay/overfitting referenced in 4.2.5; (iv) critic explained variance, a sanity check that the value function GAE relies on (3.4.4) is actually fitting; (v) the realized three-phase schedules for learning rate, clip range, and the action-limiting factor $\kappa_t$, confirmed against their configured hold/ramp/hold design. These diagnostics support the OOS results above; they do not substitute for them.
    * **4.2.5 Statistical Robustness:** identifying the "peak alpha" checkpoint on the validation blocks, characterizing the validation-decay curve as evidence of overfitting beyond that peak, and cross-referencing that decay against the training-reward divergence surfaced in 4.2.4.

* **4.3 PAA Results (planned):** no PAA inference results, trained ablations, or benchmark-portfolio runs exist yet at the time of writing this outline. This subsection fixes the experimental design, the engineering decisions behind it, and the comparison logic now, so that execution before submission is a matter of running already-specified experiments rather than re-deriving what to build or compare.
    * **4.3.1 Implementation Plan for the Three New Agents:** each of the three missing baselines gets its own subfolder under `src/agents/` with its own `run(cache, config)` entry point (e.g. `PPO_portfolio_allocator_control_ablation/`, `PPO_portfolio_allocator_cross_sectional_only/`, `PPO_portfolio_allocator_monolithic/`), following the exact convention `main.py`'s `discover_agents()`/`run_agent()` already use for every other agent — no changes to `main.py` are required, and each new agent is trainable straight from the existing training GUI. Each file re-imports and reuses as much of the existing `PPO_portfolio_allocator_weights` code as the specific ablation logically allows, rather than forking the whole module:
        - **Control ablation (SAA randomization):** the frozen SAA is never loaded or run at all during training/inference (cheaper than the full model, not just architecturally identical to it); `SAASignalWrapper`'s real forward pass through `saa_model.policy.predict(...)` is replaced by an AR(1)/random-walk noise process clipped to the SAA's action range, matching its smoothness rather than i.i.d. white noise a network could trivially learn to ignore. That noise is still committed to each asset's real, isolated shadow sub-portfolio through the existing `apply_saa_sub_actions`/`get_saa_signal_inputs` path, so shadow weight, shadow Sortino, and shadow drawdown stay populated exactly as they are for the real model — only the source of the committed action changes. `SAATokenizer`, `AttentionEngine`, `TransformerAllocatorPolicy`, `build_allocator_model`, and every training/eval callback are reused unmodified.
        - **Cross-sectional-only (no SAA signal):** implemented as the zero-fill special case of the same signal-injection wrapper (the injected "signal" column is held at a constant zero instead of AR(1) noise, and the shadow sub-portfolio is simply never traded) rather than a separately-dimensioned tokenizer. This is a deliberate decision: it keeps all three PAA-family conditions (hierarchical, control, cross-sectional-only) at identical architecture and parameter count, differing only in what occupies the SAA-signal and shadow-weight columns, which isolates the *information content* of the signal rather than confounding it with a change in model capacity. `SAATokenizer`, `AttentionEngine`, and the training scaffold are reused unmodified, same as the control ablation.
        - **Monolithic end-to-end baseline:** the only one of the three built substantially from scratch, since neither `SAASignalWrapper` nor `SAATokenizer`'s split asset/portfolio-token design apply once there is no frozen per-asset SAA to consume. `TradingEnv` itself (reward, friction model, purged splits, `EXECUTION_PORTFOLIO_WEIGHTS` mode) is reused unmodified, as are the validation/eval callbacks, since both key off environment-logged portfolio metrics rather than policy internals. New work: a single policy that supplies its own temporal memory over raw per-asset features and jointly performs timing and allocation — the design goal is to stay as close as possible to the modular system's building blocks (e.g. a per-asset LSTM feeding the same shared-attention-plus-private-heads structure of 3.5.4, rather than an unrelated architecture) so that any performance gap is attributable to the modular/monolithic split itself and not to an unrelated architectural difference, keeping it a defensible opponent to the proposed system rather than a strawman. Training compute (total env steps or wall-clock) is explicitly matched against the modular system's budget so the comparison is fair. Time-boxed and flagged as a stretch goal: if training time does not allow it before submission, its omission is reported as a limitation (Section 5.2) rather than silently dropped.
    * **4.3.2 Multi-Agent Inference and Comparison Harness:** a separate inference program (not the training GUI) that can load several trained checkpoints at once — the full hierarchical PAA, the control ablation, the cross-sectional-only ablation, the monolithic baseline, and the cash/SPY/fixed-weight-benchmark reference portfolios — and run them deterministically over the same validation-block episode plans (and, once, the held-out test block), producing the per-episode and aggregated equity-curve charts and comparison tables this chapter needs, mirroring what already exists for the SAA (4.2.2/4.2.3). This harness does not exist yet and is a prerequisite for every result in 4.3.3–4.3.4.
    * **4.3.3 Baselines and Ablations to Compare:** the comparison set the harness above must produce, in increasing order of what each isolates — engineering details for the three new agents are in 4.3.1:
        - 100% cash baseline.
        - SPY buy-and-hold, the PAA's actual reward counterfactual (3.5.3).
        - Fixed-weight benchmark portfolio (45% SPY, 20% Gold, 10% EWJ, 10% EWG, 5% Crude, 5% EWQ, 5% EWT), already defined as a diagnostic portfolio in 3.5.3 but not yet run for the PAA specifically.
        - Cross-sectional-only ablation — isolates whether the frozen SAA's temporal signal adds value at all.
        - Control ablation (randomized signal) — isolates whether any gain over the cross-sectional-only ablation comes from genuine historical information rather than merely from the extra input columns' presence.
        - Full hierarchical PAA with the real frozen SAA signal — the proposed system.
        - Monolithic end-to-end baseline — the most direct empirical test of this thesis's modular-vs-monolithic research question; included pending 4.3.1's time-box.
    * **4.3.4 Statistical Robustness PAA:** once the above results exist, apply the same treatment as 4.2.5 — peak-alpha checkpoint identification on the validation blocks, a validation-decay/overfitting analysis, and cross-referencing against PAA-specific training diagnostics (analogous to 4.2.4, e.g. attention-weight stability across checkpoints, actor/critic loss divergence per 3.5.5).

## 5. Conclusion & Future Work

* **5.1 Summary of Findings:** Validating the hierarchical approach for Financial RL and the success of "Box-based" reward shaping in stabilizing SAA agents.
* **5.2 Limitations:** Data frequency constraints, the impact of zero-friction training assumptions, and the "sim-to-real" gap.
* **5.3 Future Directions:** Multi-agent extensions for PAA coordination or integrating alternative data into the SAA temporal extraction layer.
